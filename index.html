Can lab data rank 33 polluters as well as three experts?

Srinivas & Singh (2018) ranked 33 tanneries, treatment plants and drains with expert ratings and fuzzy AHP. We found the survey's raw lab data in the paper's own sources and tried to reproduce the ranking from measurements alone.

0.75 ρ

Rank agreement with the experts, within each industrial area, using only measurements and equal weights (method B). 1 = same order.

0.26

…when failures are counted per tested parameter. Sources were tested for 1 to 17 parameters, so much of the agreement may come from panel size, not pollution.

01

What the paper does

Each source gets a score from 0 to 1 within its area (low = more polluting). Experts judged how badly each source performs on each criterion; fuzzy AHP turned the judgments into weights. We replace the highlighted step.

Inputs2012 lab tables read by 3 experts (pollution board, impact assessment, research institute).
ReplacedRatings of 33 sources on 10 criteria: discharge, ETP efficiency, failures pre/post monsoon, effluent type, river and groundwater impact, public health.
Fuzzy AHPPairwise matrices (rating gap k → 2k+1), interval fuzzy numbers, defuzzified local weights.
WeightsCriterion weights from expert importance ratings; public health highest (0.235).
RankScore = Σ criterion weight × local weight, ranked within each of 4 areas.

We reproduced the paper's own arithmetic: all 33 final scores of Table 10 recompute from its weights within 0.001, and Table 8 follows from Table 7.

03

Three ways to replace the experts

All three start from one row per source with the criteria computed from the lab data: failures against CPCB limits (pH outside 5.5–9.0; not detected = pass), treatment efficiency, flow, toxic excess. Agreement is measured within each area, because the paper ranks only within areas.

uses expert scores
A

Learn the experts

A regression model learns the paper's scores from the criteria. 12 scikit-learn models compared; PLS (2 components) wins and was chosen in 29 of 33 nested rounds. The target is normalised with training labels only.

ρ 0.74 · nested test
no expert input
B

Compute the criteria

Rank sources within their area on each criterion, scale like the paper's local weights, add with equal weights. Missing values are neutral. Every rank can be explained measurement by measurement.

05

What not to trust yet

An independent review (GPT Sol) re-ran every number; the defects it found are fixed. What remains are limits of the data.

Panel size may drive the ranking

Drains and treatment plants were tested for up to 17 parameters, most plants for 1 to 4. Counting failures per tested parameter drops agreement from 0.75 to 0.26. The experts saw the same tables, so their ranking may carry the same effect.

One survey, one year

Two sampling rounds in 2012. The rankings describe 2012, not today, and nothing here forecasts.

11 sources mapped by order

One third of the labels depend on Magarwara assignments not confirmed by values.

Groundwater: no signal

Wells have village names, not coordinates. Industrial load per locality did not predict well contamination.

Placeholders

Effluent-type classes (C5), toxicity factors and the pH range need sign-off from domain experts.

06

Data to get next

In order of value for this project.

  1. Well and outfall coordinates

    To link groundwater to single sources. Village points exist (report §3.6); Dwivedi & Vankar (2014) give 10 bore wells with GPS.

  2. 2016 joint drain survey

    UPJN/UPPCB/CPCB report: per-drain sheets with coordinates, flow, chemistry and metals.

  3. A common parameter panel

    The same parameters at every outlet, to remove the panel-size effect.

  4. Repeated monitoring

    Monthly samples for at least a year, before any forecast.
