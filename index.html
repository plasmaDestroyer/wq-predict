Replacing expert judgment with lab data: a re-analysis of the Unnao industrial wastewater ranking

Re-analysis of Srinivas & Singh (2018)1 · code and data: github.com/plasmaDestroyer/wq-predict

AbstractSrinivas & Singh (2018) ranked 33 industrial wastewater sources near Unnao, Ganges basin, with the ratings of three experts and an interval-valued fuzzy AHP. We recovered the survey's raw laboratory data from the report the paper cites2 and tested whether measurements alone reproduce the expert ranking. A transparent computed ranking with equal weights agrees with the experts at a mean within-area Spearman ρ of 0.75; a regression model trained on the expert scores reaches 0.74; standard pollution indices reach 0.28–0.69. The agreement falls to 0.26 when failures are counted per tested parameter, which suggests that the number of parameters tested at each source, rather than pollution alone, drives much of the expert ranking.

1The original method

The paper scores each source from 0 to 1 within its industrial area (four areas, low = more polluting). Three experts, from a pollution control board, an impact-assessment practice and a research institute, rated each source on ten criteria: effluent discharge (C1), treatment efficiency (C2), number of critical parameters before and after the monsoon (C3, C4), effluent type (C5), impact on the river (C6, C7) and on groundwater (C8, C9) in both seasons, and public health (C10). Pairwise matrices built from the ratings (a rating gap of k levels becomes 2k+1), interval fuzzy numbers and defuzzification give a local weight per source and criterion; the final score is the sum of criterion weight × local weight.

We reproduced the paper's arithmetic: all 33 final scores in its Table 10 follow from the printed weights to within 0.001, and its Table 8 follows from Table 7. The step this work replaces is the expert rating of each source.

The comparison also exposed errors in the paper: the "TDS 145" of Sadaf Enterprises is TSS 145 in the report, and the ACI Oils outlet BOD is 157, not 150. The paper omits the station register for two of its four areas; for 11 of those 15 sources the assignment to the paper's labels follows the report's station order and is not confirmed by values.

3Methods

All methods start from one row per source with the criteria computed from the lab data: failures against the CPCB discharge limits (pH outside 5.5–9.0; a non-detect counts as a pass), treatment efficiency (inlet versus outlet), flow, and the toxicity-weighted excess over the limits. Because the paper ranks only within areas, agreement is measured within each area (Spearman ρ, averaged over the four areas) and by whether the most-polluting source matches.

A. Learning the expert scores

Twelve scikit-learn regressors were compared by leave-one-out cross-validation, with the target normalised by the training labels only. Partial least squares with two components gave the lowest error; in a nested test (model choice inside each fold) it was selected in 29 of 33 folds.

B. Computing the criteria

Sources are ranked within their area on each criterion, the ranks are scaled to the shape of the paper's local weights, and the criteria are combined with equal weights. Missing values are neutral. No expert input is used, and each rank can be traced to measurements.

C. Adding river and groundwater

As B, plus limit-weighted river load (C6, C7) and a groundwater model per locality (C8, C9). The groundwater model did not beat the mean in a leave-one-locality-out test, so C8 and C9 remain neutral.

4Results

The computed ranking (B) agrees with the experts as closely as the model trained on their scores (A), and more closely than the standard indices. Agreement is high in three areas and lower in Site-2 (Table 2).

5Limitations

Panel size. Sources were tested for between 1 and 17 parameters; drains and treatment plants received the widest panels. Counting failures per tested parameter lowers the agreement of method B from 0.75 to 0.26. Because the experts read the same tables, the expert ranking itself may carry this effect.

  • Agreement with the experts is not evidence of a better assessment.
  • The data cover two sampling rounds in 2012; nothing here describes the present or forecasts.
  • One third of the labels depend on source assignments not confirmed by values.
  • Groundwater cannot be linked to single sources. Report village points place 17 of 55 wells, and ten further wells have GPS4, but no open source maps the individual plants. At the level of industrial areas, well TDS, fluoride, Cr(VI) and conductivity show no decrease with distance (Spearman ρ between −0.18 and 0.51).
  • The effluent-type classes, toxicity factors and the pH range are placeholders for expert sign-off.

An independent review (GPT Sol) re-ran all computations; the defects it found were corrected before these results were produced.

6Further data

  • Locations of the individual plants and CETP outfalls, to link groundwater to single sources.
  • A common parameter panel at every outlet, to remove the panel-size effect.
  • Monthly monitoring for at least one year before any forecast.
